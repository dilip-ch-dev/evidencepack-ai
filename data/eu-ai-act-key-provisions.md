# EU AI Act — Key High-Risk System Obligations (Chapter III, Section 2)

Faithful summaries of selected provisions of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the EU AI Act) governing high-risk AI systems. Each section condenses the official text; consult the published Regulation for the authoritative wording.

## Art 9 — Risk management system

Providers of high-risk AI systems must establish, implement, document, and maintain a risk management system that runs as a continuous, iterative process across the entire lifecycle of the system, requiring regular systematic review and updating. It must identify and analyse the known and reasonably foreseeable risks the system can pose to health, safety, and fundamental rights when used as intended and under conditions of reasonably foreseeable misuse, then adopt appropriate and targeted risk management measures to address them. Residual risks must be judged acceptable, and the system must be tested—including against pre-defined metrics and probabilistic thresholds—to identify the most suitable risk management measures and ensure consistent intended performance.

## Art 10 — Data and data governance

High-risk AI systems that train models with data must be developed using training, validation, and testing datasets that meet quality criteria and are subject to appropriate data governance and management practices. These practices address design choices, data collection processes and origin, relevant preparation operations such as annotation, labelling, cleaning, and aggregation, the formulation of assumptions, and examination for possible biases that could affect health, safety, or fundamental rights or lead to prohibited discrimination. Datasets must be relevant, sufficiently representative, and to the best extent possible free of errors and complete for their intended purpose, and must take into account the specific geographical, contextual, behavioural, or functional setting in which the system will be used.

## Art 11 + Annex IV — Technical documentation

Before a high-risk AI system is placed on the market or put into service, providers must draw up technical documentation and keep it up to date, demonstrating that the system complies with the requirements of Chapter III, Section 2, and giving authorities the information needed to assess conformity. The documentation must contain at least the elements set out in Annex IV, including a general description of the system and its intended purpose, a detailed description of its elements and development process, monitoring, functioning, and control information, the risk management system, and any changes made over the system's lifecycle. SMEs, including start-ups, may provide the Annex IV elements in a simplified manner using a form provided by the Commission.

## Art 12 — Record-keeping (logging)

High-risk AI systems must technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system. The logging capabilities must enable the recording of events relevant for identifying situations that may result in the system presenting a risk within the meaning of Article 79(1) or lead to a substantial modification, facilitating post-market monitoring, and supporting the monitoring of operation. For certain high-risk systems—such as remote biometric identification systems—logging must, as a minimum, record the period of each use, the reference database against which input data is checked, the input data for which a search led to a match, and the identification of the natural persons involved in verifying the results.

## Art 13 — Transparency and provision of information to deployers

High-risk AI systems must be designed and developed so that their operation is sufficiently transparent to enable deployers to interpret a system's output and use it appropriately. They must be accompanied by instructions for use in an appropriate digital or other format containing concise, complete, correct, and clear information that is relevant, accessible, and comprehensible to deployers. This information must include the identity and contact details of the provider, the system's characteristics, capabilities, and limitations of performance—including its intended purpose, accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity levels, known or foreseeable circumstances that may affect those levels, and any known risks to health, safety, or fundamental rights—as well as human oversight measures and the expected lifetime and maintenance requirements.

## Art 14 — Human oversight

High-risk AI systems must be designed and developed, including with appropriate human-machine interface tools, so that they can be effectively overseen by natural persons during the period in which they are in use. Oversight aims to prevent or minimise the risks to health, safety, or fundamental rights that may emerge when a system is used as intended or under reasonably foreseeable misuse. The oversight measures must enable the persons assigned to oversight to understand the system's capacities and limitations and monitor its operation, remain aware of automation bias, correctly interpret the output, decide not to use the system or to disregard, override, or reverse its output, and intervene in or stop the system through a "stop" button or similar procedure. For certain biometric identification systems, no action or decision may be taken by a deployer on the basis of identification unless it has been separately verified and confirmed by at least two competent natural persons.

## Art 15 — Accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity

High-risk AI systems must be designed and developed so that they achieve an appropriate level of accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity, and perform consistently in those respects throughout their lifecycle. The levels of accuracy and the relevant accuracy metrics must be declared in the accompanying instructions for use. Systems must be as resilient as possible regarding errors, faults, or inconsistencies that may occur within the system or its environment, addressed through technical and organisational measures such as backup or fail-safe plans, and systems that continue to learn after deployment must mitigate the risk of biased outputs from feedback loops. They must also be resilient against attempts by unauthorised third parties to alter their use, outputs, or performance by exploiting vulnerabilities, with measures to prevent, detect, respond to, resolve, and control attacks such as data or model poisoning, adversarial examples, and confidentiality attacks.
